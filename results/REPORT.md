### 4.2 Fixation probabilities (Kimura 1962) and their correction

| selection | RMSE vs Kimura (sex-averaged s) | within 2 SE | RMSE vs full diffusion | within 2 SE | RMSE vs deterministic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| concordant | 0.010 | 100% | 0.010 | 100% | 0.629 |
| maternal mito (male cost 0.5) | 0.005 | 100% | 0.005 | 100% | 0.675 |
| sexually antagonistic | 0.083 | 0% | 0.013 | 86% | 0.697 |

**Reading.**

* Under concordant selection the IBM follows Kimura. A female-beneficial mito variant follows the haploid formula with Ne = N/2, so it drifts twice as strongly; this is the quantitative basis of the drift effects in §3.
* Kimura's sex-averaged formula fails for strongly antagonistic selection (s_f − s_m = 0.1). Expanding the recursion gives Δp ≈ pq[s̄ − p(s_f² + s_m²)]. The second-order term vanishes for a rare allele, so invasion still depends on s̄ alone. It opposes the allele more and more as it becomes common, so fixation is less likely than Kimura predicts, and the shortfall grows with N. This is the same term that creates Kidwell's wedge (protection roughly when 0 < s̄ < s_f² + s_m²).
* The general diffusion formula, built from the full sex-specific recursion, restores agreement. This is the analytic correction the IBM asked for.

The trajectory pins down the *difference* s_f − s_m far better than either coefficient on its own; the oracle fit is best for the difference. That is an identifiability limit of additive SA selection. In small populations the oracle fit degrades because drift is unmodelled noise. The IBM-trained network is more robust there, so simulation-based inference is the safer choice for real (finite) data.

## 5. Open questions for the authors

1. **Life cycle.** We assumed haploid selection in each sex, then random union, then meiosis with recombination, and then sex assigned at random. This reproduces the poster's p* and invasion thresholds. Is it the intended cycle?
2. **'Fraction of parameter space'.** We read this as the fraction of the (a, r) rectangle (b/(1+b), b/(1−b)) × (0, 0.5]. Is that the domain and resolution used?
3. **'Away from the fixed point'.** Which initial condition was used, and how many generations were iterated? The orange plateau at M1 = 0.75 for a < 1/6 is not an attractor of the reconstructed map.
4. **Size-of-perturbation panel.** Which r was used? We assumed r = 0.1.
