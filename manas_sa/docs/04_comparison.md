# 4. Comparison: model vs papers

> **Audit note (docs/07_leakage_audit.md).** Several matches below are weaker than their verdict words suggest.
> - The sex-ratio ordering is recoverable from the raw data's variance components alone, with no simulator (an echo of the calibration).
> - The SA proportion is (1 − r)/2.
> - The LH value +0.45 comes from selecting one of 12 design points.
> - HRI magnitudes at 0.001–0.01 M at λ = 2 depend on a post-hoc collapse filter. The filter is now validated: at λ = 1 no replicate collapses (0/12), and the unfiltered λ = 1 means agree with the paper (docs/08, H18–H19).

The full machine-readable version is `results/comparison_table.csv`, regenerated by `python scripts/make_comparison.py`. Coverage is in `results/coverage.csv`.

**Claim types**
- **A** = reported in the paper.
- **B** = reproduced here.
- **C** = our extrapolation.

**Verdict words:** *matches sign / matches ordering / misses magnitude / not identified* (plus *null holds / fails* for designed nulls).

All IBM numbers use the default profile (λ = 2) unless stated. The engine, the hemiclone emulator and the estimators never see a paper number. Calibration uses line variances and within-cell noise only.

**Note (C): the SA proportion carries no information beyond r.** For scaled and centred line means, the 45° rotation gives Var(w_a)/(Var(w_a)+Var(w_c)) = (1 − r)/2 exactly. The published SA proportions equal (1 − r_w,g,mf)/2 to 4 decimals. "A higher SA proportion at FB" is therefore the same observation as "a lower r at FB".

**Flag F12.** Exact reproduction requires **untransformed** male proportions. The Methods describe an arcsine-square-root transform. With it, r = 0.382 / 0.419 / 0.236, and the male across-SR correlations are about 0.03 higher.

**CIs (F2).** The CI construction is not identified. Vial resampling attenuates the correlations, so percentile intervals sit below the estimates. The lower bounds of a "basic" interval match the published lower bounds within about 0.01; the upper bounds do not.

Out-of-sample checks in the shared regime (only line variances and within-cell CV² were calibrated):
- **Male across-SR correlations:** 0.51 / 0.68 / 0.49 against 0.557 / 0.700 / 0.542. *Matches ordering and magnitude.*
- **Female across-SR correlations:** 0.87–0.89 against 0.75–0.84. *Misses magnitude (too high).* This is expected: the model has no true genotype × sex-ratio interaction (docs/05, L2), so these correlations are below 1 only through sampling noise. The female shortfall in the data is therefore evidence of genuine G × SR for female fitness, or of extra day-level noise the model lacks (C).
- **SA proportion:** the model gives 0.45–0.46 against 0.30–0.37. *Misses magnitude.* It follows from the lower r.

|---|---|---|---|
| 39 | +0.089 | **0.15** | 0.57 |

## 4.3 Contrast reconciliation: which assumption moves the sign (C)

The direct IBM design comprises 12 points × 2 populations over:
- the fraction f_SA of new mutations that are SA, ∈ [0, 0.2];
- the concordant share of the remaining mutations, ∈ [0, 1];
- the female map length, ∈ [0.1, 2] M.

Each population received 20 calibrated 39-line assays.

| Architecture | Assay r | Compare |
|---|---|---|
| f_SA 0.06, concordant 0.93 | **+0.45** (populations +0.40, +0.51) | LH, Geeta Arun 2022: +0.40 |
| f_SA 0.02–0.08, concordant 0.24–0.55 | +0.10 to +0.14 | Ruzicka 2019 +0.15; Collet UCL +0.21 |
| f_SA 0.04, concordant 0.08 (almost all sex-limited) | −0.05 | sex-limited null (§4.2) |
| f_SA 0.09–0.18, concordant 0.48–0.83 | −0.16 to +0.22 by population | — |
| f_SA 0.10–0.14, concordant 0.12–0.39 | −0.24 to −0.27 (populations to −0.42) | Chippindale 2001 −0.30 |
| f_SA 0.19, concordant 0.85 | −0.35 (populations −0.79, +0.09) | Innocenti & Morrow −0.52; Collet UU −0.41 |
| f_SA ≥ 0.22 (first sweep) | collapse, r → −1 | — |

**Answer to "which assumption moves the sign".**
1. **The SA share of mutational input moves the sign, conditioned on how much concordant variation there is.** Within a few percent of f_SA, r goes from +0.45 to −0.35.
2. **Replicate history alone can flip the sign near the boundary.** Two populations with the same architecture gave −0.79 and +0.09.
3. **Sampling alone does not.** The assay SD of r̂ is 0.14 with 40 lines, 0.09 with 100 and 0.05 with 223. The SD between replicate populations of the same architecture is 0.15 on average, and much larger near the collapse boundary. Together these cannot carry an LH-like population (r ≈ +0.4) to −0.5. LH vs LHM therefore needs a different realised architecture or history, not just noise (C).
4. **HRI alone does not bridge the gap** (target 4, §4.5). At realistic map lengths (0.1–1 M), linkage gives population means of −0.04 to −0.13, and single populations reach −0.26. That covers Chippindale's −0.30 only marginally, and not −0.41 or −0.52. HRI also cannot make r positive.

**Emulator status: not validated.** The GP grouped-CV RMSE is 0.30 (GBM 0.29), against a replicate-population SD of 0.15, and its sensitivity indices are flat. The GP-based inversion is therefore reported as **not identified**. The direct IBM table above is used instead. More design points would not fix this, because the between-population variance itself is large.

## 4.4 A methodological finding (C): pooled-sex genotype r is confounded by intersexual differentiation

Collapse happened in 16 of 24 design populations, in all true-SA populations, and in some short-map HRI replicates at λ = 2. In these populations a class of autosomal haplotypes is carried essentially only by males: female-detrimental, male-favoured, propagated through sons. Its signature is V_f ≫ the stable value, a pooled-sex r_pop ≈ −0.94, and a hemigenome r that can be positive.

The preprint's estimator (target 4) correlates female and male breeding values over **pooled** female and male genotypes. It therefore reads allele-frequency *differences between the sexes* as negative genetic covariance. Hemiclone-type sampling (whole haploid genomes expressed in both sexes) is not confounded.

In the preprint's own setting (both sexes recombine, sex-limited mutations, N = 2,500) no replicate collapses (our λ = 1 run), so its numbers are unaffected.

The point matters for Drosophila-like systems without male recombination, and not only in collapsed populations. The LH-like populations of §4.2 are *not* collapsed (V_f/λ² ≈ 0.03), yet their pooled-sex r_pop is −0.41 … +0.01 while their hemigenome r is +0.47 … +0.73. Small allele-frequency differences between the sexes suffice. **The pooled-sex estimator and the hemiclone estimator measure different quantities when males do not recombine.**

  Compared with the paper setting (−0.086 at 0.1 M, −0.038 at 1 M), the bias is about 1.3–1.5× stronger at 0.1–1 M. The 0.01 M value rests on 4 surviving replicates and is unreliable.

**Analytic complement (C).** With r_m = 0, sex-limited selection creates *deterministic* negative LD, D* ≈ (1 − 2r̄)Δ_AΔ_B/(4r̄). It persists along a whole chromosome and does not depend on N. This is a second route to a negative indirect term alongside HRI, and explains the slightly negative r of the sex-limited LH null (§4.2).

## 4.6 Distinguishing experiment (C)

`scripts/run_distinguish.py`; figure `results/fig_distinguish_default.png`. Hemigenomes (600 per population) went through k generations of female meiosis without selection, with map lengths per real meiosis. r_LE is r with every site independently permuted, i.e. all LD removed.

| Population | r(k = 0) | r(k = 16) | r_LE | Power, 200 + 200 lines, k = 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HRI 0.01 M (paper setting, non-collapsed) | −0.20 | −0.16 | ≈ 0 | 0.08 |
| HRI 0.1 M | −0.13 | −0.08 | ≈ 0 | 0.08 |
| HRI 1 M | −0.03 | +0.01 | ≈ 0 | 0.11 |
| LH sex-limited null | −0.08 | −0.08 | +0.01 | 0.10 |
| LH shared | +0.15 | +0.25 | **+0.24** | 0.22 |
| LH true SA (collapsed) | −1.00 | −1.00 | **−0.75** | 0.97 |

**Result (C).**
- In every HRI and sex-limited population, *all* of r is LD: r_LE ≈ 0.
- Sixteen generations of female meiosis remove very little of it.
- The proposed recombination experiment therefore has **no power** (≤ 0.18 even with 200 + 200 lines).

The reason is general. LD that persists in a population against recombination at rate c each generation sits between loci with c ≲ 1/(N_e·generations of persistence). A lab treatment of k meioses multiplies it by (1 − c)^k, which is negligible unless k·c ≳ 1. **HRI-type LD cannot be broken in a feasible experiment**, so a recombination test cannot separate SA from HRI.

In the LH regimes the LD term has the *opposite* sign to the direct term:
- shared: r rises from +0.15 to +0.24 when LD is removed;
- true SA: −1.00 → −0.75.

LD therefore *masks* part of the concordant (and SA) signal. The replacement discriminator is N-dependence (docs/05 §5.2.2). At the preprint's per-generation parameters, r = −0.128, −0.144 and −0.042 at N = 625, 1,250 and 2,500. The direction is as HRI predicts but the trend is not monotone, and about 31 replicate populations per N would be needed. The practical criterion is therefore **magnitude and sign**:
- HRI gives only negative r. Population means are |r| ≲ 0.15 at 0.1–1 M; single populations reach −0.26 at 0.1 M and −0.19 at 1 M.
- LH's positive r cannot be HRI-driven.
- Chippindale's −0.30 is within HRI's single-population range only at short maps (not identified).
- The values −0.41 and −0.52 exceed realistic-map HRI.

## 4.7 Robertson layer: within- vs cross-sex covariance of relative fitness

This substitutes for target 3, which is inaccessible; trait-level covariances are **not identified**. The quantity is the ratio of cross-sex to within-sex covariance of relative fitness, which gives the cross-sex share of each sex's predicted response (sex-specific FTNS).

| Ratio | Data (B, MB / E / FB) | Model, shared regime | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Female (cross / within) | 0.90 / 0.90 / 0.51 | 0.23 / 0.22 / 0.15 | matches sign; matches ordering (FB lowest); misses magnitude |
| Male (cross / within) | 0.15 / 0.18 / 0.12 | 0.04 / 0.04 / 0.04 | matches sign; misses magnitude |

- **Direction (data).** Female response depends heavily on cross-sex covariance. Male response is dominated by within-sex variance, about 6× the female line variance.
- **Model.** It reproduces the direction and asymmetry but not the size. The size scales with r, and the shared regime's r is too low.
- **Thesis Ch. 4 (A).** Male persistence traits are positively genetically correlated with female fitness (latency −0.47 to −0.26, P1 +0.18 to +0.36, P2 +0.27 to +0.42), and these correlations weaken at MB. That is the trait-level counterpart of a positive cross-sex term. The model has no traits, so this is **not identified**.
