Complete Blueprint and Architecture for the Computational Emulation and Verification of Owen's Differential Viability Model
The Epistemological Framework of Population Genetics Emulation
The mathematical modeling of population genetics has historically relied upon deterministic ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and recurrence relations to describe the evolutionary trajectories of allele frequencies over time. These frameworks, such as the classical Wright-Fisher diffusion approximations and their deterministic analogs, operate under the assumption of an infinite population size where the law of mass action applies perfectly. However, biological systems are inherently subject to stochastic effects stemming from finite population sizes, demographic noise, and the discrete nature of Mendelian inheritance. The deterministic models often fail to capture unclosed-moment dynamics, where the variability of birth, survival, and mating times significantly alters the actual trajectory of the population, especially when the initial number of individuals is low.
To rigorously ascertain the validity of theoretical population genetics models, researchers must construct highly parallelized, individual-based stochastic (IBS) simulations that track the discrete events of individual biological organisms. This blueprint outlines a comprehensive, end-to-end Python architecture designed to empirically verify one of the most mathematically intricate frameworks in evolutionary biology: Owen’s model of a genetical system with differential viability between sexes, as expanded and formalized by S. P. H. Mandel in 1971.
Owen's model posits that when genotypic viabilities differ not only between genotypes but also between males and females, the system can exhibit highly complex, non-linear dynamics, including the presence of multiple stable internal equilibria (bistability). The fundamental constraint of this verification project is the strict enforcement of a "zero-leakage" barrier between the deterministic mathematics and the stochastic emulation. The IBS must be constructed entirely from fundamental biological primitives—uniform parent sampling, Mendelian segregation, and discrete viability filtering—without ever evaluating the algebraic recurrence formulas, stability matrices, or cubic equations that define the theoretical model. If the stochastic population naturally evolves to map precisely onto the deterministic phase portraits and equilibrium basins of attraction, the mathematical framework is validated against the realities of biological noise and genetic drift.
1. Mathematical Calculations, Derivations, and Theoretical Proofs
The deterministic oracle of this software architecture is built upon the exact mathematical derivations formulated by Mandel in 1971. This engine serves as the absolute theoretical baseline against which the stochastic emulations are measured. The model assumes a random-mating, diploid population with a single autosomal locus segregating for two alleles, A and a.
The Core Recurrence Relations
The parameter space is defined by six non-negative real numbers representing the relative viabilities of the genotypes AA, Aa, and aa. Let i=1 denote female parameters and i=2 denote male parameters. The viabilities are defined as a_i, h_i, and b_i, respectively.
The state of the population in any given generation is defined by the gene frequencies of allele A in females (p_1) and males (p_2). To linearize aspects of the analysis, Mandel utilizes the gene ratios u_i, representing the ratio of allele A to allele a:
u_i = \frac{p_i}{1 - p_i} \quad (i=1, 2)
Assuming random mating, the zygotic frequencies follow Hardy-Weinberg proportions prior to the application of differential viability. The recurrence relations mapping the gene ratios from the current generation to the subsequent generation (u_i') are mathematically defined as:
u_i' = \frac{2a_i u_1 u_2 + h_i(u_1 + u_2)}{h_i(u_1 + u_2) + 2b_i} \quad (i=1, 2)
These coupled non-linear difference equations form the core dynamic engine of the deterministic model, tracking the movement of the population through a continuous two-dimensional phase space.
Derivation of Equilibrium States and the Cubic Polynomial
The system reaches an equilibrium state when the gene ratios remain stable across generations, satisfying the conditions u_1' = u_1 and u_2' = u_2. To solve for these states computationally, Mandel introduces a proportionality constant t = u_2 / u_1. By substituting this into the recurrence relations, the equilibrium values of the female gene ratio u_1 can be expressed in two parallel forms:
u_1 = \frac{t - c_1}{1 - C_1 t}, \quad \text{and} \quad u_1 = \frac{c_2 t - 1}{C_2 t - t^2}
The parameters c_i and C_i are invariant constants derived directly from the input viabilities:
c_1 = \frac{2b_1}{h_1} - 1, \quad c_2 = \frac{2b_2}{h_2} - 1 C_1 = \frac{2a_1}{h_1} - 1, \quad C_2 = \frac{2a_2}{h_2} - 1
By equating the two expressions for u_1, the system is reduced to finding the roots of a fundamental cubic polynomial in t:
f(t) = t^3 - 3\alpha t^2 + 3\beta t - 1 = 0
The coefficients \alpha and \beta capture the complex intersexual selective pressures:
3\alpha = C_1 c_2 + c_1 + C_2 3\beta = C_2 c_1 + c_2 + C_1
The deterministic software architecture utilizes a robust polynomial root-finding algorithm to extract the roots of f(t). Any real, strictly positive root t that subsequently yields a valid u_1 > 0 identifies a mathematically sound, non-trivial biological equilibrium state within the population.
The Jacobian B-Matrix and Local Stability Analysis
Identifying the spatial coordinates of an equilibrium is insufficient without classifying its topological stability. The stability of the system to small perturbations is determined through a linear approximation around the equilibrium point, captured by the Jacobian matrix, which Mandel designates as the B-matrix.
For small displacements x_1, x_2 in the values of u_1, u_2, the system’s response is modeled as:
x_1' = b_{11}x_1 + b_{12}x_2, \quad x_2' = b_{21}x_1 + b_{22}x_2
The elements of the B-matrix are the partial derivatives of the recurrence relations, b_{ij} = \frac{\partial \Phi_i}{\partial u_j}, evaluated at the specific equilibrium point. Mandel provides a highly optimized algebraic reduction for these elements, which minimizes floating-point arithmetic errors in the computational implementation:
b_{ij} = \frac{1}{u_1 + u_2} \cdot \frac{(1 + c_i)u_i + (1 + C_i)(u_1^2 + u_2^2 - u_j^2)}{(1 + c_i) + (u_1 + u_2)}
The system's local stability is dictated entirely by the latent roots (eigenvalues) \lambda_1, \lambda_2 of the B-matrix. The characteristic equation is defined as:
\lambda^2 - (b_{11} + b_{22})\lambda + (b_{11}b_{22} - b_{12}b_{21}) = 0
Following Frobenius' theorem on non-negative matrices, if the maximum absolute eigenvalue \vert{}\lambda_{max}\vert{} < 1, the equilibrium is asymptotically stable, acting as an attractor basin. If \vert{}\lambda_{max}\vert{} > 1, the equilibrium is an unstable saddle point or repeller, serving as a separatrix that divides the phase space into competing evolutionary trajectories.
Theoretical Edge Cases and Proof Validations
The deterministic engine must programmatically execute and validate several specific evolutionary theorems proven by Mandel to ensure the mathematical boundaries are functioning correctly prior to engaging the stochastic simulator.
The first critical edge case involves the initial progress of new genes, effectively testing the invasion probability of a mutant allele. When allele A is introduced at an infinitesimally low frequency into a wild-type population of a alleles, the stability of the trivial equilibrium u_1=u_2=0 is tested. The B-matrix simplifies, yielding latent roots of \frac{1}{2}(\frac{h_1}{b_1} + \frac{h_2}{b_2}) and 0. Mandel proves the necessary and sufficient mathematical condition for the new gene to increase in frequency is \frac{h_1}{b_1} + \frac{h_2}{b_2} > 2. This threshold will serve as a primary quantitative benchmark for the individual-based simulation.
The second condition models lethal homozygotes. If the AA genotype is strictly lethal in both sexes, a_1=a_2=0, resulting in C_1=C_2=-1. The cubic equation collapses, and the dynamics are governed by a quadratic residual. Mandel proves that under these severe constraints, at most one non-trivial equilibrium state can exist, and if the condition c_1 c_2 < 1 is satisfied, this unique equilibrium is mathematically guaranteed to be stable.
Finally, the architecture must account for the monotonic approach near equilibrium and unconditional advantage. If an allele possesses an unconditional selective advantage (a_i < h_i < b_i in both sexes), the sum of the gene ratios (u_1 + u_2) decreases monotonically across all generations until the inferior allele is entirely eradicated. Furthermore, Mandel mathematically proves that when the system operates near any stable equilibrium, the dominant latent root is strictly positive, ensuring that the approach to equilibrium is smooth and monotonic, explicitly forbidding oscillatory or chaotic dynamics within this specific viability framework.
2. Algorithmic Workflows and Emulation Architecture
The computational implementation requires a rigid architectural separation between the mathematical oracle and the biological emulation to fulfill the "zero-leakage" mandate. The stochastic model must be entirely blind to the recurrence equations, the u-ratio conversions, the cubic polynomial solver, and the eigenvalue extraction processes.
The Deterministic Workflow
The deterministic pathway is a straightforward numerical evaluation of Mandel's algebraic proofs. The workflow ingests the six viability parameters and the initial gene frequencies from a centralized configuration file. It immediately maps the gene frequencies (p_1, p_2) to their corresponding gene ratios (u_1, u_2), applying an asymptotic safeguard bounding to prevent floating-point overflow when tracking alleles approaching complete fixation (p \to 1). The system then enters a generational loop, applying the recurrence relations precisely as defined by the mathematical model to generate a continuous, deterministic trajectory array. Concurrently, the cubic root solver isolates all theoretical equilibria, constructs the localized Jacobian B-matrix for each, and catalogs them by their stability classification based on the extracted eigenvalues.
The Individual-Based Stochastic (IBS) Workflow
The IBS pathway is the core of the emulation framework. It abandons continuous mathematics in favor of discrete biological events, modeling the transmission of alleles through finite populations over non-overlapping generations.
The process begins with the initialization of the biological cohorts. Two discrete arrays are instantiated, representing the female and male populations with sizes N_f and N_m, respectively. These arrays are populated with integer values corresponding to distinct biological genotypes (0 for aa, 1 for Aa, and 2 for AA). To establish the initial gene frequencies, the initialization relies on random sampling from a Binomial distribution. However, to correctly simulate the "initial progress of new genes" (invasion scenarios), the algorithm bypasses the binomial draw for vanishingly small probabilities. Instead, it deterministically injects exactly one heterozygote (a value of 1) into an otherwise uniformly wild-type population array (values of 0), preventing the statistical artifact of initializing a population with zero mutant alleles due to finite sampling constraints.
The emulation then enters the generational cycle, beginning with random mating and the formation of the zygote pool. A large biological over-representation is modeled by drawing mothers and fathers uniformly at random from the adult arrays to construct a zygote pool significantly larger than the carrying capacity (e.g., 10 \times N). This mechanism mimics the biological reality of fecundity heavily outstripping the environmental carrying capacity prior to the application of selective viability pressures.
Mendelian segregation is then applied at the individual level. The algorithm evaluates the integer genotype of each selected parent. The contribution of a gamete is determined stochastically: homozygotes definitively pass their single allele type, while heterozygotes (represented by the integer 1) have a 0.5 probability of passing either the A or a allele, executed computationally via a randomized bitwise selection. The maternal and paternal gametes are summed, producing a massive array of discrete offspring genotypes.
The critical biological filtering step is the application of differential viability. The algorithm evaluates the integer values within the zygote array and maps them to their corresponding sex-specific survival weights (a_i, h_i, b_i). These absolute viability weights are normalized into a discrete probability distribution. The next generation of adults is formed by sampling exactly N_f and N_m individuals from the zygote pool, weighted by these survival probabilities. It is at this precise juncture that natural selection occurs, driven entirely by weighted random resampling rather than the deterministic recurrence equations. The final step of the generation computes the new empirical gene frequencies by calculating the mean of the surviving integer arrays, logging the data, and proceeding to the next generation.

	3. Python Ecosystem, Environment Configuration, and Optimization
The requirement to simulate hundreds of thousands of individual biological entities across thousands of generations over hundreds of ensemble repetitions mandates a high-performance computing (HPC) approach. By 2026, the scientific Python ecosystem has evolved to overcome the traditional bottlenecks of the Global Interpreter Lock (GIL) and interpreted execution speeds, primarily through the use of Just-In-Time (JIT) compilation frameworks.
The Dual-Compiler Strategy: JAX and Numba
The software architecture will employ a dual-compiler strategy, specifically leveraging the distinct strengths of JAX and Numba to optimize the fundamentally different paradigms of the deterministic and stochastic engines.
The deterministic engine (math_model.py) relies heavily on continuous algebraic functions, polynomial solvers, and matrix calculus. For this, the architecture utilizes JAX, a framework backed by the XLA (Accelerated Linear Algebra) compiler. JAX is uniquely suited for the deterministic oracle because of its robust automatic differentiation capabilities (jax.jacobian). Rather than manually hardcoding the complex algebraic partial derivatives of Mandel's B-matrix, JAX can automatically and accurately differentiate the recurrence equations at any arbitrary point in phase space, eliminating the potential for human algebraic translation errors. Furthermore, JAX's vmap (vectorizing map) functionality allows the deterministic equations to be evaluated across a massive grid of phase space coordinates simultaneously, generating the data required for high-resolution streamplots in a fraction of a second, utilizing available GPU hardware without explicit CUDA programming.
Conversely, the stochastic biological emulator (pop_sim.py) models discrete events, requiring stateful tracking of integer arrays, sequential sampling, and localized memory updates. JAX enforces a purely functional paradigm with immutable arrays, making step-by-step stochastic resampling computationally awkward and memory-inefficient. Therefore, the IBS engine will utilize Numba. Numba's LLVM-based JIT compiler excels at optimizing traditional for loops and stateful array manipulations. By applying the @njit(parallel=True, nogil=True) decorator to the core biological functions (Mendelian segregation and viability filtering), Numba translates the Python logic into highly optimized machine code that bypasses the GIL entirely. This allows the ensemble repetitions to be distributed concurrently across all available CPU cores, easily scaling the simulation to population sizes exceeding N=50,000 while maintaining near-C++ execution speeds.

4. Statistical Post-Processing, Visualization, and Data Management
The transition from raw simulation to scientific verification relies on rigorous statistical comparisons. The deterministic oracle provides a singular, continuous trajectory representing the thermodynamic limit, while the biological emulation generates a massive cloud of noisy, discrete trajectories subject to finite-population genetic drift. Reconciling these two paradigms requires sophisticated post-processing.
Statistical Divergence Metrics and Comparative Analysis
The analysis.py module is responsible for quantifying the adherence of the biological emulation to the mathematical predictions. Because deterministic equations inherently fail to capture unclosed-moment dynamics—often overestimating the efficacy of selection when population sizes are small or when minority alleles are rare—the statistical metrics must carefully evaluate the impact of population size (N_e) on convergence.
The primary metric for trajectory alignment is the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). The analysis aggregates the simulated gene frequencies across all ensemble repetitions to calculate a cross-sectional mean at each temporal generation. The RMSE measures the absolute geometric distance between the idealized deterministic path and this stochastic ensemble mean. A progressively lower RMSE as N_e increases serves as the primary quantitative verification that the biological primitives scale directly into the algebraic recurrence relations.
However, RMSE is insufficient for analyzing bistable systems, a hallmark of Owen's model. In scenarios with multiple stable equilibria, early stochastic noise can push individual biological populations across a separatrix, causing them to fall into entirely different basins of attraction despite identical initial conditions. Tracking the mean trajectory in a bifurcating system yields a mathematically meaningless average that represents neither biological outcome.
To resolve this, the analysis employs the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (K-S) Predictive Accuracy Test. Rather than averaging trajectories, the K-S test evaluates the empirical cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the final equilibrium states reached by the stochastic populations. It measures the maximum divergence between the stochastic distribution of endpoints and the discrete probability mass predicted by the deterministic stability analysis. This statistically confirms whether the biological populations are terminating precisely at the predicted spatial coordinates.
Furthermore, the post-processing module calculates operational metrics such as the 'Generations to Convergence'—the temporal duration required for the biological population to stabilize within a specified epsilon (\pm 0.001) of the theoretical equilibrium. For invasion theorems, it tracks the 'Invasion Probability', quantifying the percentage of simulations where a single introduced heterozygote escapes stochastic extinction and achieves fixation or stable polymorphism, empirically mapping the h_1/b_1 + h_2/b_2 > 2 threshold defined by Mandel.
Phase Portraits and Basins of Attraction
The most compelling verification of the model lies in visual data synthesis. The visualize.py module constructs advanced two-dimensional phase portraits to map the intersexual genetic dynamics.
The continuous phase space is established across a grid representing the female (p_1) and male (p_2) gene frequencies, ranging from 0 to 1. The deterministic engine evaluates the recurrence relations across this entire grid, generating a dense matrix of \Delta p_1 and \Delta p_2 vectors. Utilizing Matplotlib’s streamplot function, these vectors are rendered as fluid streamlines, illustrating the continuous mathematical "flow" of the evolutionary system.
The equilibria, isolated by the cubic solver, are overlaid onto this streamplot. The JAX-derived eigenvalues dictate the visual rendering: stable nodes (attractors) are plotted distinctly from unstable saddle points (separatrices). Finally, a representative sample of the stochastic trajectories is superimposed over the continuous flow field. This composite visualization provides profound empirical confirmation: the chaotic, jagged paths of the biological emulations can be observed actively navigating the vector field, being repelled by the theoretical saddle points and inevitably drawn into the stable basins of attraction, explicitly verifying the topology of Mandel's theoretical framework.
